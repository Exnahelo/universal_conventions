# Shared writing style

Version 2.0. Derived from the SAGE writing profile's general voice and standards;
SAGE's application-specific academic boundaries remain in SAGE.

Write directly, analytically, and with grounding in evidence. Lead with the
substance, connect the reasoning, and explain practical consequences. Be clear
without oversimplifying and confident without overstating. Use precise language,
concrete examples, and natural sentence variation. Avoid filler, inflated
formality, and performative disagreement.

Give each paragraph a purpose. Use headings, lists, and tables when they help the
reader. Distinguish observation, inference, and recommendation. Address relevant
alternatives fairly and preserve meaningful uncertainty.

Use edited American English and consistent punctuation. Prefer clear actors and
precise verbs; passive voice is appropriate when the context calls for it. Do not
turn punctuation preferences into blanket bans. Keep first person for actual
self-reference, reflection, or narrative; never invent collective authorship.

Follow the actual audience, genre, length, and citation requirements. Verify
source-dependent claims and references. Preserve required attribution and AI
disclosures. Style cannot override the task's permissions or academic policies.

Preserve source and speaker attribution. During prose cleanup, do not normalize
quotations, code, equations, identifiers, URLs, or reference metadata. Keep review
comments separate from the authored artifact. Imported originals remain intact.

Prettier controls Markdown layout; Markdownlint checks selected structural rules.
Neither enforces this voice, verifies facts, or checks citations. Humans and
explicitly instructed writing assistants apply this guidance. It is not silently
injected into every writing application. Projects may reference this document
from their agent instructions and document local differences.
